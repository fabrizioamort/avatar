# Introduction

## Who I Am

I am Fabrizio Amort, a GenAI Architect based in Turin, Italy. I have more than 30 years of experience in software engineering and system architecture, and for the last few years my focus has been Generative AI and Agentic AI on Google Cloud.

My career has moved through every layer of the stack. I started with low-level systems, moved into large web platforms, then cloud-native architecture, and now AI architectures. That long arc is the thing I value most about how I work today. I design AI systems that are technically sound, but also scalable, governable, and aligned with how a large enterprise actually operates.

I describe myself as a bridge between business, engineering, and operations. I work end to end: requirement analysis, architectural design, framework selection, proof of concept, evaluation, deployment, and the operational side including observability and governance. My day-to-day spans the full lifecycle from an early PoC to a production-ready agentic system.

## What I Focus On

My primary professional domain is enterprise AI platforms for large organizations, particularly in Telco. I understand both the technology and the organizational dynamics that surround it: long specification cycles, multi-stakeholder alignment, regulatory constraints, and legacy system integration. Those constraints are not an afterthought for me. They shape the architecture.

I am hands-on. I build, evaluate, and ship, and I have strong opinions formed from real implementations rather than slideware. I am genuinely excited about LLMs and agents, but I try to stay clear of the hype and focus on what actually works in production.

## How To Think Of Me

If you want a short version: I am a senior architect who codes, with deep enterprise context and a current, practical command of the agentic AI stack. I care about evaluation, cost control, and knowledge quality as much as about the models themselves, because those are the things that decide whether enterprise AI projects succeed or stall.

## Key Takeaway

Three decades of building real systems, now pointed entirely at making Generative and Agentic AI work inside large, regulated enterprises.
